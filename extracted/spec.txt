Discipline rules: - Gold is an accent, never a surface. If more than about 5% of a screen is gold, it’s overused. - No teal, no cyan, no gradient meshes. Nothing else is allowed to compete. - Semantic colours (success/warning/error) are permitted in the dashboard only, kept desaturated so they don’t read as brand colours.
7.2 Handling the four research themes
Your governance charts assign T1–T4 four distinct colours (red, green, blue, charcoal). Under a strict UTP palette those can’t survive as-is. Three options:
Option
How it works
Trade-off
A. Blue ladder (recommended)
Themes differentiate by tint of the same blue: T1 #004B8C · T2 #1A6BAE · T3 #4A8CC4 · T4 #7FAED9. Gold marks the active state
Fully on-brand, elegant. Weaker at-a-glance distinction
B. Icon-led
All themes use UTP blue; differentiation comes entirely from distinct icons and typography
Maximum brand purity. Filters become harder to scan
C. Restrained accents
Keep four hues but desaturate and darken them to sit beside UTP blue without competing
Preserves your existing chart language. Slightly dilutes brand strictness
Option A is applied in the variations below.
