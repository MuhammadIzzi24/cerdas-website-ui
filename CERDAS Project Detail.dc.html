The problem

When a fault occurs on a medium-voltage feeder, the control room sees a cascade of alarms and a rough indication that something is wrong somewhere on a twelve-kilometre run. Locating it means dispatching a crew to drive the line. On the partner network that took forty-seven minutes on average, and the customers at the far end of the feeder waited the whole time.

The data to do better already existed — nine years of fault records, topology that was mostly accurate, and SCADA telemetry from the substations. What did not exist was a method that could work with it. Published approaches assume dense sensing along the feeder and a clean network model. A real distribution network has neither, and the records themselves carry mislabelled causes and missing restoration times.

SENTINEL-GRID set out to learn the structure that matters from what is actually recorded, and to hand the result to an engineer in a form they can challenge.
