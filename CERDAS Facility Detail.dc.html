What it is for

PETRA-1 exists because the computational work at the centre outgrew the point where a workstation under a desk was an honest answer. It runs three kinds of job: large-scale simulation, model training on datasets that will not fit in memory, and long parameter sweeps that need to run unattended for days.

It is a shared facility in the real sense — allocation goes to the case, not the rank. A doctoral student with a well-argued request routinely gets more cluster time than a professor with a vague one, and the allocation record is visible to everyone in the centre.

Roughly a fifth of capacity is reserved for external users — other universities, agencies and industry partners on a costed basis. At 83% utilisation the cluster is close to full, and a second procurement is under review.
